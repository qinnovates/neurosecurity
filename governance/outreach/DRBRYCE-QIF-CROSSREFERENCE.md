## 5. Lessons Learned

**Structural asymmetry: threat enumeration vs. defense.** TARA has no mitigation/defense field on any technique record. 165 entries buy threat-model breadth, not controls. This isn't a flaw to hide — it's worth naming explicitly, because Paper 1 is an attack-*and*-defense survey and the mismatch would otherwise surface as an unstated gap in any comparison table.

**Self-refereed validation is real but limited.** Neurowall's 78.6–100% detection numbers are measured against a 14-pattern attack suite authored by the same person who built the detector, on synthetic data. This is honestly disclosed in your own docs (VAL-001's limitations section) — keep disclosing it that way. It's a real first-measured-baseline in a field with none, which is valuable; it is not a validated detector.

**Version drift and internal contradiction are the biggest avoidable risk in the entire portfolio.** Three different technique counts (165/109/99), a registry that miscounts its own tactic/domain fields, a public README overclaim, a false FIRST.org status claim, and a direct disclosure-status contradiction — none of these are hard to fix, and all of them are the kind of thing a careful outside reader finds in the first few minutes. The research process for this report caught them because multiple independent agents were told to verify rather than trust. That's a good practice to keep: before any future external-facing document ships, do a dedicated internal-consistency pass, not just a content pass.

**The CONFIRMED status label conflates two different things**: "the underlying phenomenon is documented in the literature" and "this has been demonstrated as an attack against a neural interface." Kujirai 1993 (TMS-SICI) is real, replicated neuroscience — it is not a confirmed attack. This single conflation is the highest-severity finding in the whole review, because it's the kind of thing that's checkable in about ninety seconds by exactly the kind of reader you're trying to reach.
