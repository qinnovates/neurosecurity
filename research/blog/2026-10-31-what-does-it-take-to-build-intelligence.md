## The pyramid

This is a conceptual model, not a measured result or a standard. Each level depends on the ones below it.

1. **Physical foundation:** silicon, energy, processors, sensors.
2. **Connectivity and infrastructure:** networks, protocols, cloud, edge computing.
3. **Data and signals:** acquisition, storage, processing, provenance.
4. **Software and computation:** operating systems, runtimes, APIs, algorithms.
5. **Models and algorithms:** machine learning, neural networks, prediction.
6. **Autonomy and intelligence:** reasoning, planning, adaptation, decision-making.
7. **Application and experience:** robotics, AI products, interfaces, brain-computer interfaces.
8. **Human flourishing:** creativity, accessibility, discovery, agency, human potential.

The top level is the point of the whole structure. The other seven are means to it.

## Why security isn't a capstone

Every layer brings its own risk. Hardware can be compromised. Data can be manipulated. Models can be exploited. Intelligent systems can make decisions we can't trust.

So a security layer placed at the top doesn't work. In the film it dissolves into pathways that run through every level: cybersecurity, AI safety, privacy, resilience and governance.

A sixth pathway appears when technology starts interacting directly with the nervous system: neurosecurity. That's the work we do at Qinnovate, where our frameworks are proposed research tools, not adopted standards.
