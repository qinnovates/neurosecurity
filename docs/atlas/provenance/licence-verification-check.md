# Independent check of `licence-verification.md` (TARA Brain Atlas P2)

Date of every fetch below: **2026-10-09**. Web research only: `curl` against publisher pages, raw files and APIs (OSF, Zenodo, figshare, Dryad, GitHub, Crossref, DataCite). No account, login or agreement. No imaging volume or archive was downloaded; where a notice could only live inside an archive, I read the zip directory and the small text members by HTTP range request (NextBrain `3dAtlas.zip`, MNI `mni_icbm152_nlin_asym_09c_nifti.zip`, PAM50 release zip). I am not a lawyer. "Interpretation" marks every place where the verdict rests on a reading and not on the words.

Bar applied: primary text read this session, permits public redistribution of derived files and commercial use; share-alike flagged; account, click-through, agreement or obligations beyond attribution = NEEDS-KEVIN; silent, self-contradictory or unreadable = DO-NOT-SHIP.

## Result in one paragraph

One SHIP verdict is wrong: **Mindboggle-101 DKT31**. The atlas's own OSF component says the atlases are under CC BY-NC-ND 3.0. Three SHIP verdicts are right only on interpretation and do not clear a words-only bar: **Yeo 2011**, **Schaefer 2018** (MIT "software" wording applied to data files) and **Melbourne Subcortex** (no word on commercial use). **HCP1065** holds as share-alike, with one unresolved reading and a trap in the same release. Every other SHIP verdict was confirmed word for word. All four spot-checked negative verdicts hold; none is too strict. Every DOI cited for these sources resolves to the title and authors the review gives.

## Notes

**A. Allen: is the exception real?** Yes, in words. The grant is made by the Allen Institute on its own download host, in the README that sits in the same directory as `annotation.nii.gz` and `annotation_full.nii.gz`, and the same sentence is in the Allen forum post (author flagged "AIBS Internal Users", last edited 2022-08-31). "These materials" covers the directory: both annotation volumes and `examples/voxel_count/voxel_count.csv`, which holds the structure ids, acronyms, names, colours and parent ids. The download is a public URL (HTTP 200, no agreement). Two cautions. (1) The general Allen terms still say non-commercial "unless it is otherwise stated in these Terms or agreed to in writing"; the README is a written statement by the Institute but it is not "in these Terms". I read a specific later CC BY grant by the rights holder as controlling (interpretation, low doubt). (2) The CC BY grant covers the files in that directory. The example scripts fetch the ontology from the Allen Brain API; anything pulled from the API or the web atlas instead of this directory falls back under the general non-commercial terms. Take the structure table from `voxel_count.csv` only. The README also sends users to McGill for the underlying MRI template and its terms; the template is not in the directory.

**B. CIT168 Reinforcement Learning.** The words exist and I agree with SHIP. The doubt: all three OSF nodes in the project (`r2hvk`, `jkzwp`, `hksa6`) carry the identical field (CC BY 4.0, year 2017), and for `hksa6` the authors' own LICENSE.txt and wiki say BY-SA. So the field looks project-wide and was not corrected where the authors meant something stricter. For `jkzwp` no stricter text exists anywhere I could read (component file list, component wiki, parent wikis, the Scientific Data article). If the build wants zero exposure, ship the CIT168 RL meshes in their own file and treat them as share-alike; that satisfies both readings at no cost.

**C. HCP1065.** The publisher's own words for the tract atlas are CC BY-SA 4.0 plus "additional acknowledgment". Reading the link target as incorporating the whole HCP agreement is possible but is not what the sentence says, and the same release page uses different words ("License: the WU-Minn HCP open access data use term (4)") for the fibre templates, which shows the author knows how to apply the HCP terms when he means to. So: tract atlas files are share-alike with the HCP acknowledgement sentence as a required credit, not a courtesy. Do not take `ICBM152_adult.*` or `1065_subject_tract_in_nifti.zip` from that release: the first is under the HCP redistribution clause by the publisher's own statement, the second is per-subject data. Whether the author was entitled to put HCP-derived data under CC BY-SA is his obligation under the agreement he accepted; no text binds a downloader who accepted nothing (interpretation).

**D. Yeo 2011 and Schaefer 2018.** MIT does not plainly cover the label files. Its grant is over "this software and associated documentation files". What supports the reviewer's reading: the root README says the repository "is a package that provides ... 2. Brain parcellations and algorithms" and then "See our LICENSE file for license rights and limitations (MIT)", and there is no carve-out anywhere in the tree. What weakens it: label volumes and `.annot` files are data, not software or documentation; the copyright line is "2016 Computational Brain Imaging Group", while the Yeo 2011 parcellation was made in 2011 at a different institution and is also shipped inside FreeSurfer under the FreeSurfer licence. I found no other statement of terms by the authors: the FreeSurfer wiki page for the parcellation (live site timed out; read from a web.archive.org capture dated 2026-10-04, which is a mirror, not primary) has no licence wording. This is SHIP on interpretation. A one-line written confirmation from the CBIG group would turn it into words.

**E. Melbourne Subcortex.** Redistribution of the atlas and modified copies: granted in words. Commercial use: silent as a word, covered only by "without restriction". The removal of "sublicense, and/or sell" from an otherwise MIT sentence is the reason not to treat this as word-clean. Displaying meshes on a commercial site is "use ... publish and distribute"; selling the atlas is not granted. The only condition is a citation.

**F. Mindboggle.** The file the review clears, `OASIS-TRT-20_jointfusion_DKT31_CMA_labels_in_MNI152_v2.nii.gz`, lives in the atlases component, and that component's own text links its licence to CC BY-NC-ND 3.0. NC forbids the commercial site; ND forbids meshing, relabelling or dropping the subcortical labels. The CC BY 4.0 statement the review quotes covers the individually labelled brains (`Cortical_VolumeLabels_[GROUP]`, `SurfaceLabels_[GROUP]`, `FreesurferFiles_[GROUP]`), which are per-subject data in subject space, not an atlas. The OSF licence field on the component says CC BY 4.0, which makes the record self-contradictory; under the bar that is DO-NOT-SHIP either way. This removes the review's "finer option" for a V1 proxy (pericalcarine) and a premotor proxy (caudal middle frontal). The cortex then rests on the Allen gyral labels alone, unless the Mindboggle authors confirm CC BY for the atlas files in writing.

**HCP pass-through, per source (publisher's own text):**
- CIT168 RL: none. "Based on a midspace template derived from Human Connectome Project structural MRI."
- CIT168 amygdala: none as a condition; the wiki prints the HCP acknowledgement sentence under "Acknowledgements".
- Neudorfer: none. "990 subjects derived from the HCP1200 data release were included in this study."
- Melbourne Subcortex: none in the licence. README mentions HCP only for restricted behavioural data.
- HCP1065: "Using them requires additional acknowledgment."
- Schaefer (Kong 2022 ordering only): none.
- Allen, AHEAD, MASSP, AAN, NextBrain, MNI, H01, Yeo 2011: not HCP-derived by their own descriptions.

All of these HCP-derived atlases share one residual risk that no page resolves: the HCP agreement asks its signatories to redistribute derived data "under these same Data Use Terms", and the atlas authors chose CC licences instead. A downloader who never accepted the HCP terms is not a party to them (interpretation). Carrying the HCP acknowledgement sentence beside every HCP-derived file is cheap and I would make it standard.

## Verdicts I would change

1. **Mindboggle-101 DKT31 cortical labels: SHIP to DO-NOT-SHIP.** The atlases component's own wiki links the atlas licence to CC BY-NC-ND 3.0; the review quoted the parent project's statement about per-subject labelled brains and missed this one. Even on the kindest reading the record contradicts itself (component licence field CC BY 4.0, component text NC-ND 3.0). Details in note F.

No other verdict changes. Three verdicts keep their label but should carry a plain "interpretation" mark in the build spec, because the grant is not in the words:

2. **Yeo 2011 and Schaefer 2018:** MIT over "software and associated documentation files" applied to data files (note D).
3. **Melbourne Subcortex:** commercial use rests on "without restriction"; "sell" and "sublicense" were removed (note E).
4. **HCP1065:** share-alike is in words; that the HCP requirement is acknowledgement only rests on reading one linked word (note C). Add to the verdict: never take the fibre templates or per-subject files from the same release.

Smaller corrections to the review text, no verdict change:
- NextBrain: the in-zip readme is now read. It has no notice and no template space. The colour table has 916 rows, so "333 regions" is not what the shipped label table shows; count the labels present in `atlas.labels.argmax.nii.gz` before stating a number.
- Allen: say in the verdict that the structure table must come from `voxel_count.csv` in the CC BY directory, not from the Allen API.
- CIT168 RL: record that the OSF licence field is the sole statement and that the same field is contradicted by author text in the sibling component.
- Diedrichsen: the per-atlas JSON does not leave the question open so much as point at the NonCommercial file; the README's CC BY sentence is the outlier.
